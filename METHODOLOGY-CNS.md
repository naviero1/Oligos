# Methodology — OligoTox-CNS Dataset

Methodology and provenance documentation for **OligoTox-CNS**, a curated,
openly-releasable, per-measurement dataset of oligonucleotide **central nervous
system toxicity**, for the NIH/NCATS Oligonucleotide Toxicity (OligoTox) Open
Data Challenge, **Phase 2 (Data Generation Phase)**.

It is the companion to `METHODOLOGY.md` (the kidney dataset) and follows the same
discipline: an **in-silico curation** of already-published data, so the "materials
and methods" below describe source identification, extraction, harmonisation,
grading, provenance and quality control — how the dataset was *assembled and
computationally processed*.

Data dictionary, controlled vocabularies and the grading rubric are in
**`schema-cns.md`**. The source registry is **`sources/SOURCES-CNS.md`**.
Summary statistics are generated by `scripts/dataset_stats_cns.py`, not typed, so
they cannot drift from the data.

---

## 1. Scope, and why it is scoped this way

The challenge brief names its toxicities of interest as *"hepatotoxicity, kidney
toxicity, thrombocytopenia, complement activation, coagulopathy, immunotoxicity,
**chronic neurotoxicity**, and **hydrocephalus**"*, and adds:

> *Given the availability of large data sets focused on acute neurotoxicity,
> specifically alterations of neuronal electrical activity, submissions focused on
> this topic will be considered a lower priority than other toxicities of interest.*

Three consequences shaped every later decision:

1. **Chronic neurotoxicity and hydrocephalus are the targets.** Hydrocephalus in
   particular had no curated dataset anywhere that we could find, and it is one of
   only two CNS endpoints NCATS names.
2. **Acute neurotoxicity is in scope but must be declarable.** It is the most
   strongly sequence-dependent CNS signal in the literature and therefore the most
   useful single predictor available — discarding it would have thrown away the
   only large panels that pair sequences with graded CNS outcomes. So it is
   included and every row carries `endpoint_domain` and `challenge_priority`,
   letting a consumer remove the deprioritised class with one predicate instead of
   discovering it inflated the record count.
3. **Neuronal-electrical-activity readouts are the lowest-value class** and are
   flagged as such. 181 rows carry `low_acute_electrophysiology`; they are present
   only because they are the matched in-vitro arm of an in-vivo panel (§4.5).

**Granularity.** One row = oligo × model/subject × CNS region × delivery ×
dose/concentration × readout. One oligo at one dose measured for neurofilament
light *and* ventricular volume is two rows.

**Driving domain fact.** CNS oligonucleotide toxicity separates into distinct
mechanisms that a single label would blur: an acute, sequence-dependent,
hybridisation-*independent* behavioural syndrome minutes after intrathecal or
intracerebroventricular dosing; a chronic neuroinflammatory and neurodegenerative
process (astrogliosis, microgliosis, neuro-axonal injury); and CSF-dynamics
disturbance (hydrocephalus, raised intracranial pressure). The schema captures
`endpoint_domain`, `reversibility` and `exposure_duration` so these remain
separable rather than collapsing into one "neurotoxic" flag.

## 2. Data model

Two normalised UTF-8 CSV tables joined on `oligo_id`. `data/cns_oligos.csv`
deliberately reuses the **identical 17-column layout** as the kidney dataset's
`data/oligos.csv`, so the two datasets can be unioned or compared without
re-mapping. `data/cns_measurements.csv` extends the measurement layout with the
CNS-specific columns `cns_region`, `endpoint_domain`, `challenge_priority` and
`reversibility`, and grades on `neurotox_grade` 0–3.

Missing/unknown values are the literal string `TBD` — never guessed, never
imputed as zero.

## 3. Source identification

Sources were pursued in a deliberate order of evidentiary and legal strength:

1. **US regulatory documents** — FDA prescribing information (via DailyMed) and
   FDA application records. Public domain; values reproducible without restriction.
2. **EMA documents** — EPAR assessment reports, SmPCs, PRAC/CHMP records,
   and Direct Healthcare Professional Communications. These proved decisive for
   hydrocephalus, which is documented in EU material that has no US equivalent.
3. **US granted patents** — public domain, and uniquely valuable because they pair
   a formal sequence listing with per-compound tolerability tables.
4. **ClinicalTrials.gov posted results** — public-domain US government data whose
   adverse-event module is already at our grain (per-arm counts with denominators).
5. **Open-access primary literature** (PMC), with redistribution rights read from
   each article's own licence statement (§7).
6. **Trial publications and case series** behind paywalls — used only where the
   number could actually be read, and marked as abstract-derived when it could not.

Reviews were used to *find* primary sources and to cross-check, not as the cited
source for a value.

## 4. Extraction

Extraction ran as nine independent lanes, each working to a written contract
(`notes/cns/EXTRACTION_CONTRACT.md`) that fixed the schema, vocabularies, grading
rubric and — above all — the no-fabrication policy. Lane outputs are retained as
JSON under `notes/cns/extractions/` so any row can be traced back to the lane and
the reasoning that produced it.

### 4.1 The no-fabrication policy

**A value may only be written if it was read out of a document fetched during
curation.** Values recalled from model memory are not acceptable provenance,
however confident. `sequence_5to3` and every toxicity `readout_value` are `TBD`
unless explicitly sourced, and compounds lacking published CNS data were omitted
rather than padded.

This is not a formality. During curation an extractor drafted a European
marketing-authorisation number from memory that was wrong in its last three
digits, and caught it only by reading the number back out of the fetched SmPC. A
number that looks right is the failure mode that matters here, because nothing
downstream will flag it.

### 4.2 Mechanical verification where it was possible

Where a source was machine-readable, rows were generated by script rather than
transcribed, with an assertion that the record at the cited locus is the record
the row claims:

- **ClinicalTrials.gov** rows are emitted by indexing into the downloaded JSON at
  the exact path stored in `source_table`, asserting the MedDRA term at that index
  matches the row's, and only then computing numerator, denominator and
  percentage. All 131 assertions passed, and the whole lane was re-derived from a
  fresh API fetch and reproduced 131/131.
- **Patent** sequences were reconciled against every length declaration printed in
  the same table row — start/stop span, gapmer motif sum, chemistry-code length,
  linkage-code length + 1 — and rejected on any disagreement.
- **WHO INN** sequences were derived by deterministic parse of the longhand
  chemical name, with the parser first required to reproduce five already-known
  sequences character-for-character, and each result cross-checked against the
  published molecular formula, where the phosphorus count fixes the length.

### 4.3 Extraction hazards encountered

Recorded so the next round does not repeat them:

- **Sequence-listing parsing is treacherous.** A naive base-run regex can skip a
  residue whose modified form renders as the wrong character in a PDF text layer,
  then absorb letters from the following text, producing a plausible but wrong
  sequence of plausible length. Always reconcile against a declared length.
- **Table cells wrap.** Reading patents through an HTML mirror truncated a 20-mer
  to 10 nt, because the mirror splits a long cell across table rows. Caught by the
  same length reconciliation.
- **Case is data, not styling.** In gapmer rows, case encodes chemistry (upper =
  MOE/cEt/LNA wing, lower = DNA gap). It must be resolved from the text layer;
  judging it from rendered pixels at x-height is unreliable.

### 4.4 Values read from figures

Some sources publish per-compound results only as plots. Where a bar was
unambiguous the group mean was digitised and the row says so; where it was not,
`readout_value` is `TBD` and the direction and significance carry the signal. A
qualitative row is a legitimate record; a fabricated precision is not.

### 4.5 A scope decision made explicitly

Hagedorn 2022's supplementary table carries calcium-oscillation scores for 1,825
ASOs but in-vivo scores for 181. Only the 181 were ingested, so that every
in-vitro row added is half of a **matched in-vitro/in-vivo pair on the same
molecule with a published sequence** — which is the form the challenge's
"bridge animal data to in vitro systems" request actually takes. Ingesting all
1,825 would have made the *deprioritised* endpoint class three-quarters of the
dataset while adding nothing to the paired set. The remainder stays available in
the cited CC-BY supplement. The reasoning is written into
`scripts/ingest_calcium_panel.py` rather than left implicit.

## 5. Assembly, identity resolution and de-duplication

`scripts/assemble_cns.py` merges the lanes into the canonical tables. Three parts
carry real risk and are worth stating:

**Identity resolution.** The same molecule is extracted independently by several
lanes under different names (tofersen / BIIB067 / ISIS 666853 / QALSODY). Records
are unioned **only** on globally-identifying tokens — sponsor development codes,
INN stems, brand names — plus a secondary rule requiring *identical name and
identical sequence*.

The failure modes are wildly asymmetric, and the whitelist exists because of a bug
that was caught: an earlier revision unioned on any token containing a digit,
which fused one paper's "ASO3" with an unrelated compound of the same paper-local
label from a different study, attaching one molecule's toxicity to another's
sequence. Over-merging is indistinguishable from fabricated data once it is in the
CSV; under-merging leaves a visible, harmless duplicate. The rule is therefore a
whitelist and under-merging is the accepted failure mode.

**Conflicts are surfaced, not resolved silently.** Where lanes disagree on a field
the winner is chosen by source strength and the loser is written into the merged
row's `notes` as `CONFLICT(field)=…`. 55 such disagreements are recorded.

**Sequence notation is separated from sequence fact.** Lanes legitimately differ in
notation without differing in substance: T versus U for the same nucleobase (a
2′-MOE-5-methyluridine is thymine as a base but uridine as a nucleoside), and flat
versus chemistry-encoding case. Sequences are compared on canonical bases; a
notation difference is recorded as a variant, the case-encoded spelling is kept
because case carries the gapmer design, and base letters follow one authority so
the table speaks a single alphabet. **This mattered:** the same defect in the
kidney table meant inotersen and eplontersen — the same nucleobase sequence, one
the GalNAc-conjugated successor of the other — were spelled in different
alphabets and did not compare equal. That has now been corrected there too.

**Zero is a value.** An early revision of the assembler used the idiom
`str(s or "")`, which, because `0` is falsy in Python, silently rewrote every
grade-0, zero-dose and zero-incidence row as `TBD` — erasing precisely the
negative controls the dataset exists to preserve. Fixed, and called out here
because the failure was invisible in every summary statistic except the grade
histogram.

## 6. Grading

Each measurement carries an ordinal **`neurotox_grade` 0–3** assigned from the
reported endpoint per the rubric in `schema-cns.md`. Grades are flagged
`grade_provisional` pending scientific sign-off.

Four grading disciplines were enforced across all lanes:

1. **Direction is a grading input.** Neurofilament light is both an efficacy and a
   toxicity biomarker. A *fall* after a SOD1- or HTT-lowering ASO is intended
   pharmacology (grade 0); a *treatment-emergent rise over baseline* is
   neuro-axonal injury (grade 2+). `scripts/qc_cns.py` refuses any NfL row with
   `effect_direction = TBD`.
2. **The comparator carries the grade.** An adverse-event rate at or below the
   control arm is grade 0 however alarming the term. In one tominersen study CSF
   NfL rose in *every* arm including placebo — Huntington's natural history over
   13 weeks — so the lower-dose arms, rising less than placebo, are correctly
   grade 0 despite an absolute increase.
3. **Futility is not toxicity.** Several programmes stopped because the drug did
   not work. A trial halted for futility is a grade-0 `programme_dosing_stop` row,
   not a severe-neurotoxicity row, and the two are distinguished explicitly.
4. **Marker-only readouts cap at grade 2.** A single biomarker or in vitro
   activity measure cannot establish the rubric's grade-3 criteria of
   degeneration, paralysis, intervention or death.

Where a source uses its own tolerability scale, the mapping onto 0–3 is stated in
the row's `notes` and in the lane's extraction notes, so it is reproducible rather
than editorial.

## 7. Provenance and redistribution

Every row carries `source_id`, `source_ref` and `source_table` (the exact table,
figure, label section, claim or API path).

`source_ref` is normalised to a canonical identifier — NCT number, US patent
number, DOI or PMID — with the citation as written preserved in `notes`. Before
normalisation the same trial appeared under several strings, which inflated the
apparent source base and defeated cross-lane de-duplication, since the duplicate
detector keys on the reference.

`redistribution` is **read from the licence statement inside the archived source
document**, not assumed from the journal:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `public_domain` | US patents, FDA and EMA documents, ClinicalTrials.gov. Reproduce freely. |
| `cc_by` | Source article is CC-BY, which permits reproducing raw values with attribution. |
| `summary_stat` | Numbers quoted from a source whose licence does not permit bulk reproduction. |
| `verify` | Rights unresolved; must be settled before release. |

The `cc_by` distinction is deliberate and conservative in both directions.
Upgrading true CC-BY sources is what allows whole per-oligo panels to be
republished verbatim. **CC-BY-NC and CC-BY-ND sources are not upgraded**, because
reproducing their tables inside a CC-BY dataset would conflict with the
non-commercial and no-derivatives terms; their facts are quoted as
`summary_stat` instead.

## 8. Quality control

`scripts/qc_cns.py` runs after every assembly and enforces:

- **Schema conformance** — column sets, and every categorical value against the
  `schema-cns.md` enums.
- **Referential integrity** — `measurements.oligo_id` → `oligos.oligo_id`, no
  orphans, no duplicate primary keys.
- **Range checks** — `neurotox_grade ∈ {0,1,2,3}`, numeric doses, boolean fields.
- **Provenance completeness** — `source_id`, `source_ref` and `source_table`
  present and non-`TBD` on every row.
- **Endpoint policy** — no NfL row may leave `effect_direction` unset;
  `challenge_priority = low_acute_electrophysiology` if and only if
  `readout_category = electrophysiology`.
- **Sequence sanity** — IUPAC bases only, case-insensitively (case encodes
  chemistry, so a case-sensitive validator would reject correct rows), with
  length cross-checked against `length_nt`.

Two further invariants were added after verification showed what they catch:

- **Death is grade 3, without interpretation.** Any row recording a non-zero
  death count must be graded 3. This is the one grading rule needing no
  judgement, so drift here means a grading pass has gone wrong somewhere less
  obvious.
- **A group mean of n integer scores is a multiple of 1/n.** An ordinal score of
  4.1 is arithmetically impossible for a four-animal group, and betrays a value
  read off a bar's height rather than off the plotted per-animal points. Scoped to
  values the notes declare as figure-derived.

Two QC rules were themselves corrected during development, which is worth
recording because a bad check is worse than no check. The "grade 0 with an
increase in an injury readout" rule fired on every *rescued* phenotype — more
neurons, longer neurites — and on tominersen NfL rows where the drug arm rose
less than placebo; it now excludes readouts where a rise is recovery and rows
citing a control comparison. The 1/n rule initially fired on exactly-transcribed
spreadsheet values and on ordinary two-decimal rounding. A check that cries wolf
trains the curator to ignore it.

## 8a. Independent adversarial verification

The dataset was then verified by agents instructed to **refute rather than
confirm**, to default to `REFUTED` where they could not confirm, and to re-fetch
the cited source rather than rely on recall. Sampling was stratified by risk —
every hydrocephalus row, every grade-3 row, all figure-digitised values, the
largest lane, plus samples of the clinical, in-vitro and animal strata.

**614 verdicts over 11 batches: 229 CONFIRMED, 223 CONFIRMED_MINOR, 149 REFUTED,
13 UNVERIFIABLE. 253 rows corrected.** Full detail in `VERIFICATION-CNS.md`.

Three mechanisms made this safe to apply, and each exists because of a way it
could have gone wrong:

1. **Verdicts are re-keyed onto rows by content, never by `measurement_id`.**
   Assembly renumbers ids, and the verification batches were exported from an
   earlier assembly, so applying a correction by a stale id would have written it
   onto an unrelated row. Two verifiers independently warned that their batch ids
   no longer matched the live tables; because the applier re-keys on canonical
   source, locus, readout, oligo, value and dose, the drift was a non-event.
   Verdicts whose re-key is ambiguous are reported and skipped, never guessed.
2. **Corrections are type-checked before being applied.** Verifiers legitimately
   write prose recommendations — `"neurotox_grade": "2 unless source_ref is
   amended to co-cite …"` — which are useful arguments and catastrophic cell
   values. 16 such were recorded as `ADVISORY` in the row's notes instead of being
   written into grade and dose columns.
3. **Verifier claims are leads, not findings.** At least one reported a defect
   that had already been fixed, working from a stale export. The most serious
   correction of the round was accepted only after reading the source document
   directly.

### What verification changed

- **A fabricated exposure figure was removed from 17 rows.** They attributed
  "median 119.4wk (range 4-212wk)" to the Qalsody EPAR; full-text extraction of
  that 181-page document returns **zero** hits for either number. The EPAR gives
  Pool ABCL1 — the n=147 population every affected row uses — a median total
  exposure of 148.4 weeks and a maximum treatment duration of 245 weeks.
- **Grade 3 fell from 199 rows to 133.** The dominant error was *scale
  anchoring*: cutoffs set at a fraction of a scale's numeric range rather than at
  what the scale's own text says the value means. A rodent score of 4 ("does not
  move forward after lifting but has an upright posture") had been graded severe
  on a scale whose severe band begins at 6 ("does not respond to pinch but is
  breathing") and whose 7 is death.
- **A large block of rows carried an inherited claim.** In one patent, an 8-week
  behavioural score turned out to be an *animal-loss* readout rather than a
  persisting deficit: across all 92 compounds the scores sit on a 7×k/4 grid for
  four-mouse groups, every zero-scoring group has unasterisked four-mouse RT-PCR
  data, and every group scoring the full 7 has no tissue at all. Those rows keep
  grade 3 — but as mortality, which is what they actually mean.
- **`reversibility` is now `not_assessed` on 1,929 of 2,331 rows.** This is a
  correction, not a gap. A large panel's rows claimed `reversible` although the
  animals were observed for one hour after a single dose and never re-examined —
  the paper's recovery language is explicitly fenced to a different cohort of
  nine ASOs. Separately, adverse-event *incidence* loci had been promoting "did
  not lead to discontinuation" into `reversible`; those tables carry no outcome
  field at all.
- **A rights over-claim was corrected.** Rows citing a Direct Healthcare
  Professional Communication were marked `public_domain`; a DHPC is issued by the
  marketing-authorisation holder, not the regulator, so it is not a government
  work.

### What verification says about the extraction

The extraction was mostly right, and wrong in a structured way. Arithmetic
fidelity was high — registry counts, denominators, table values and sequence
strings verified almost universally, and one verifier re-derived an entire
131-row lane from a fresh API fetch with 131/131 agreement. Where rows failed,
they failed at *interpretation*: a grade band anchored to the wrong thing, a
comparator chosen badly, a claim of recovery imported from adjacent prose. That
is the more tractable class of error, because it can be corrected by rule rather
than by re-extraction — which is what was done.

## 9. Independent variables

Design predictors per `cns_oligos.csv`, and the axes they are meant to support:
modality (`oligo_class`), backbone chemistry, sugar modifications, gapmer design,
conjugate, phosphorothioate count, length, and sequence. The patent-derived panels
are especially valuable here because they contain **matched design series** —
compounds differing in exactly one variable. The dataset contains, for example, a
pair sharing an identical 20-mer nucleobase sequence and differing only in
backbone (full-PS versus PS/PO mix), where the mixed-backbone version scores lower
on every matched CNS readout.

## 10. Known limitations

- **Grades are provisional**, pending subject-matter review. Verification checked
  whether each grade follows from its source under the stated rubric; it cannot
  substitute for a toxicologist's judgement about the rubric itself.
- **Verification was a stratified sample, not a census.** Rows outside the sampled
  strata carry the systematic risks named in `VERIFICATION-CNS.md` in proportion
  to how far they resemble the rows that were checked.
- **Recovery was rarely assessed.** `reversibility` is `not_assessed` on 1,929 of
  2,331 rows, because most sources simply never looked. This limits how much the
  dataset can say about the acute-versus-chronic distinction on any individual
  row, even though that distinction is central to the endpoint — and it is a
  concrete gap that new data generation could close cheaply, by adding a recovery
  arm to studies that already run.
- **Grey literature cannot be re-verified.** Rows citing sponsor medical-affairs
  slide decks are marked `redistribution=verify` and are the weakest evidence in
  the dataset; one of them is load-bearing for a headline hydrocephalus figure.
- **Hydrocephalus is thinly and unevenly sourced, and must not be overstated.**
  The evidence is almost entirely clinical: no published animal study was found in
  which an oligonucleotide *caused* hydrocephalus, and no in vitro model of CSF
  dynamics for oligonucleotide toxicity exists at all. The nusinersen signal is
  seriously confounded — spinal muscular atrophy itself carries an elevated
  hydrocephalus rate, and the pivotal trials recorded zero events in either arm.
  The tominersen exposure-dependent ventricular-volume finding is the better
  anchor. Raised intracranial pressure and hydrocephalus also **dissociate** and
  are kept as separate readouts for that reason.
- **In vitro human-based systems remain under-represented**, and this is a finding
  rather than an omission. Systematic searching found almost no published study
  whose *purpose* is oligonucleotide toxicity in human iPSC-derived neural cells;
  nearly every human in vitro row had to be mined from the tolerability or control
  arms of efficacy papers. That gap is precisely what the challenge exists to
  close, and it is the clearest argument for what should be generated next.
- **Source concentration.** A large share of rows comes from a small number of
  high-yield documents (two Ionis/Biogen patents and one CC-BY mouse panel).
  Errors in those sources propagate widely; they were correspondingly prioritised
  for verification.
- **Species imbalance.** Rodent rows dominate, reflecting where the published
  CNS-delivery toxicology actually is. The animal-to-human translation gap should
  be modelled, not ignored.
- **Paywalled sources.** Some clinically important papers could not be read in
  full. Rows resting on abstracts say so in `source_table` and should be verified
  before release.
- **Oligos without measurements.** A small number of oligo rows carry verified
  identity and sequence but no measurement yet; they are retained as a reference
  identity table for the next ingestion round.

## 11. Reproducibility

Every value is traceable to a citable locus, and the pipeline is scripted
end-to-end: `scripts/ingest_calcium_panel.py` (one deterministic lane),
`scripts/parse_inn_chemical_names.py` (sequence derivation, with a self-test),
`scripts/assemble_cns.py` (merge), `scripts/qc_cns.py` (validation),
`scripts/build_merged_cns.py` (analysis view), `scripts/dataset_stats_cns.py`
(documentation statistics). Source documents are archived under `sources/cns/`,
so the derivations can be re-run without network access.

## 12. Intended use for predictive modelling

The two-table design exposes granular sequence, chemistry and design predictors
against graded, per-condition CNS outcomes. Beyond a single "is it neurotoxic"
label, the dataset is built to support three distinctions that matter clinically:
**acute versus chronic** (via `endpoint_domain`, `exposure_duration` and
`reversibility`), **efficacy versus toxicity** on biomarkers that serve as both
(via `effect_direction`), and **in vitro versus in vivo translation** (via the
matched pairs in §4.5).
