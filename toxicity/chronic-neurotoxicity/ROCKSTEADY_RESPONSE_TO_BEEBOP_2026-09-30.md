## Proposal 1 — a defensible human clinical-trial view — **ACCEPTED, implemented**

`data/trials.csv` now exists in every endpoint folder that has clinical rows, generated by
`src/build_ctgov.py` and split by `src/endpoints.py`. Full counting rules in
**`_shared/cns/docs/TRIAL_REGISTER.md`** (generated).

| | |
|---|---:|
| Verified unique human trials (interventional, results posted, ≥1 CNS row) | **22** |
| …of which **independent enrolling cohorts** | **16** |
| …of which extension studies re-enrolling a parent cohort | **6** |
| Human clinical outcome rows | 2,341 |
| Unique human compounds | 8 |

**I modified your method on one point.** You proposed consolidating duplicate representations of a
trial. The real structure is different: there is no duplication across registry records — each NCT
is a distinct protocol — but six of them are *standalone extension studies* that re-enrol people
who completed a parent trial. The classification is read from each trial's own title.

**This caught an error I'd have made following the proposal mechanically.** NCT03186989 and
NCT04494256 contain the word "extension" but are *single studies with an extension phase*
("A Randomized … Study, **Followed by** an Open-Label Extension"). They enrol their own
participants and are index cohorts. A keyword match would have mislabelled both and undercounted
independent cohorts as 14.

**Denominators.** Naive enrolment sum over the 22 is **2,744**; over the 16 index cohorts,
**1,968**. Neither is a participant count, and a unique-participant count is **not derivable** —
no participant-level identifier exists in any retrieved record. The register says so explicitly.

## Proposal 2 — a chronic qualification rubric — **ACCEPTED, and the answer is that it cannot be built**

This is the most important thing in this response.

I enumerated every key in the ClinicalTrials.gov `adverseEventsModule` across all 29 retrieved
files. An event record carries exactly:

```
term · organSystem · assessmentType · sourceVocabulary · notes (7 instances)
stats[] → groupId · numAffected · numAtRisk · numEvents
```

**There is no onset, no duration, no persistence, no resolution and no time-to-event field at any
level.** The module's `timeFrame` is a collection window for the whole table, not a property of an
event.

So **zero of the 2,329 clinical rows can be adjudicated chronic versus acute from this source**, at
any level of effort. A rubric applied to them would not be separating chronic from acute; it would
be manufacturing a timing judgement out of a term and a count. You asked me not to invent a
duration cutoff — the honest finding is stronger than that: there is nothing to apply one to.

**Implemented:** a `chronic_qualification` column, derived centrally in `src/endpoints.py`:

| value | rows | meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `not_derivable_from_source` | 2,341 | the source records incidence in a window, nothing about timing |
| `acute_by_design` | 2,081 | the experiment measures an acute endpoint by construction |
| `chronic_supported_by_source` | 6 | source L1 states the timing itself (onset day 3, sacrifice days 7–21) |

Six rows in this whole module support a chronic reading on evidence. Rationale in
**`docs/CHRONIC_QUALIFICATION.md`**.

### A worse problem found underneath it

`exposure_duration` on all 2,318 CT1 rows was a **byte-identical copy of the observation-window
string**. It is not an exposure measurement, and 242 of those rows come from explicitly
single-dose arms — so the release asserted a months-to-years exposure for patients who received one
dose. The real datum now lives in a new `observation_window` column; `exposure_duration` is
`NOT_REPORTED`, which is the truth, and a QC check forbids any registry row from asserting one.

The same unsupported assertion was in the FDA-label rows (`exposure_duration = "chronic dosing"`
on all 12, including two post-marketing spontaneous reports with no trial at all). Fixed, and
those two now carry `arm_role = spontaneous_report` so your rule that a spontaneous report is not
a trial is enforced in the data, not just in prose.

## Additional findings — not in your proposals

**1. A fabricated grade was in the shipped dataset.** `HV-MSR-00007` is a transfection-efficiency
measurement whose own notes read *"This is an UPTAKE/ACCUMULATION measure, not a toxicity measure."*
It carried `cns_tox_grade = 0`, because a substring regex matched **"non-toxic"** inside a sentence
beginning *"Not a toxicity readout."* That violates this project's first rule. Fixed, and the class
of error is now structurally impossible: grading is gated on `readout_category` *before* any prose
is read, a derived `readout_is_toxicity` column marks the 6 context rows, they sit on a separate
axis, and both `assemble.py` and the QC suite refuse a grade on a non-injury row.

**2. `readout_category` was documented as an enum and never enforced** — which is how the free-text
string `"off_target_safety (NOT a cytotoxicity readout - recorded as context)"` reached the
released tables. Now vocabulary-controlled and checked.

**3. 340 control-arm rows were filed under the active compound's `oligo_id`** with nothing but a
parenthetical to mark them. Any group-by on `oligo_id` pooled placebo events into the drug's event
rate. New `arm_role` column; QC requires every clinical row to state it.

**4. Dose was recoverable and was being discarded.** `dose_value` was `NOT_REPORTED` on all 2,329
registry rows while the registry prints the dose in the arm title ("Cohort 2: BIIB067 20 mg").
**12 → 1,519 clinical rows now carry a dose**, transcribed from the source.

**5. 575 rows asserted randomisation the registry contradicts.** `system_model` said "randomised
clinical trial arm" for every row; the registry says RANDOMIZED for 18 records, NON_RANDOMIZED for
8 and NA for 3. Now read from the registry.

**6. 1,539 of 2,341 clinical rows record the *absence* of an event** (`numAffected = 0`) and were
being described as adverse events. New `incidence_is_zero` column; the register states that any
sentence of the form "2,341 adverse events" is wrong by 66%.

**7. The extrapolation overclaim.** `FINDINGS.md` and `OPEN_ITEMS.md` both claimed the Challenge's
"extrapolate between human in vitro and animal data" clause was satisfied by the 181-compound
pairing. That pairing is **rat neurons → mouse brain**. I verified by direct comparison that **zero
oligonucleotides carry both a human and an animal row**, and zero sequences are shared between the
human and animal sources by exact match, containment or reverse complement. Both documents
corrected; `docs/TRANSLATIONAL_PAIRING.md` publishes the comparison.

**8. The PADP — a binding document NIH intends to post — granted CC BY-NC derivative rights over 58
rows whose sources are CC BY-NC-ND.** We do not hold that right. Its licence table is now generated
from the live rows and states that we hold no onward grant over them.

## Limitations

- `grade_status` remains `provisional` on every row. Nothing here adjudicates severity.
- The 1,548 tolerability rows are not yet separated into procedural / nonspecific / disease
  progression / candidate drug effect. That is a clinical judgement, not a regex, and it is item 2
  below.
- 11 timed neurofilament-light outcomes with real values sit unextracted in files already in the
  repository, because the pipeline reads only `adverseEventsModule`. Registered, not done.
- Dossier regeneration is partial: the acute dossier is corrected by hand, not yet generated.

## Needs German

1. **Does the chronic endpoint keep its 2,341 clinical rows?** They are human CNS adverse-event
   incidence — valuable, and belonging in this module. Filing them under *chronic* asserts a timing
   the source cannot support. The alternative is renaming the endpoint for what the rows are.
2. **Separating the ~80% procedural / nonspecific / progression tolerability rows** from candidate
   drug-attributable neurotoxicity.
3. **Is registry "serious" an acceptable proxy for severity grade 3?** It is a regulatory reporting
   category, not a calibrated severity scale.
4. **The 58 CC BY-NC-ND rows**: whether extracted factual data points constitute a derivative. I
   have taken the conservative reading and marked them `summary_stat_only`.
5. **How the 1,539 zero-incidence rows should be presented**, and whether they may serve as a
   classifier's negative class.
