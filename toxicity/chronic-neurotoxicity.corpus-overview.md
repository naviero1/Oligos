# OligoTox-CNS — A Curated CNS-Toxicity Dataset for Oligonucleotide Therapeutics

A curated, openly-releasable, **per-measurement** dataset of **central nervous
system toxicity** signals for therapeutic oligonucleotides, built for the
**NIH/NCATS Oligonucleotide Toxicity (OligoTox) Open Data Challenge, Phase 2**
(Data Generation Phase).

Companion to the **OligoTox-Kidney** dataset in this repository (`README.md`).
Same two-table design, same no-fabrication discipline, same provenance rules —
a second named endpoint rather than a second project.

The deliverable is a **dataset**, not a model: openly-releasable,
well-documented, and reproducible.

---

## Why CNS, and why scoped this way

The challenge brief names its toxicities of interest as *"hepatotoxicity, kidney
toxicity, thrombocytopenia, complement activation, coagulopathy, immunotoxicity,
**chronic neurotoxicity**, and **hydrocephalus**"* — and then adds a
qualification that most submissions will miss:

> *Given the availability of large data sets focused on acute neurotoxicity,
> specifically alterations of neuronal electrical activity, submissions focused on
> this topic will be considered a lower priority than other toxicities of interest.*

So this dataset is built **chronic-neurotoxicity-first and
hydrocephalus-first**. Acute neurotoxicity is included — it is the most strongly
sequence-dependent CNS signal in the literature, and the large panels that pair
*sequences* with *graded CNS outcomes* are all acute — but every row declares
which bucket it is in through `endpoint_domain` and `challenge_priority`. A
consumer can drop the deprioritised class with a single predicate rather than
discovering after the fact that it inflated the record count.

Only **181 rows (7.1%)** are neuronal-electrical-activity readouts, and they are
present only because they are the matched in-vitro arm of an in-vivo panel on the
same molecules (see *Matched pairs* below).

**1,246 grade-0 rows are deliberate — and 54 of them are not negatives.** A dataset that records only positives
teaches a model that every oligonucleotide is neurotoxic. Negative controls
include systemically dosed oligonucleotides with no CNS exposure, adverse events
occurring at or below their placebo arm, and studies that looked for a CNS effect
and found none.

But a grade of 0 means four different things, and only two of them are a measured
negative. The derived `ascertainment` column says which, and `negative_eligible`
turns it into one predicate: **1,186 grade-0 rows are eligible as negatives and 60
are not.** The 60 are kept, with their sources, and excluded from every negative
count. They break down as 41 rows where no ascertainment basis could be
established from the row's own source fields, four whose adverse-event table lists
only terms above a frequency cut-off, four where the source reports no CNS
endpoint at all, three whose entire finding is a *warning not appearing* in a
label, seven measured in patients given no oligonucleotide at all, and one where
the compound *reduced* the endpoint.

Three of those groups are worth naming. The label-absence rows are the clearest
case: one cites a label section stating that carcinogenicity studies *had not been
conducted*, which had been recorded as grade 0 — an endpoint never assessed,
standing as evidence of no toxicity. A document's silence is not a measurement.
The disease-background rows measure hydrocephalus in SMA patients who received no
oligonucleotide; they are a baseline the exposed rows must be read against, not a
negative for any compound. And the single therapeutic-reduction row is an ASO that
*prevented* hydrocephalus in a transgenic mouse model — perfectly well measured,
and an efficacy result rather than evidence that the compound is safe.

### Human evidence first

`study_type` cannot carry a human-first presentation: it has three values, so a
rat cortical culture and a patient-derived iPSC neuron are both `in_vitro`, and a
registry-posted trial table, a label's pooled safety summary and a single case
report are all `clinical`. The derived `evidence_class` column splits them, and
the per-endpoint dossiers break it down class by class.

| | Rows |
|---|---:|
| **Human — all classes** | **661** |
| …human clinical trial-derived | 379 |
| …**human laboratory** (in vitro / ex vivo) | 116 |
| …other human evidence (label, postmarketing, case, cohort, review) | 166 |
| **Animal — all classes, supporting material** | **1,877** |
| …animal in vivo | 1,696 |
| …animal laboratory (in vitro) | 181 |

Trials are counted in the per-endpoint registers, never from rows:
**29 distinct verified human trials** across the two CNS endpoints — 27 under
chronic neurotoxicity, 12 under hydrocephalus, 10 of them in both, which is why
the two registers must not be added. A further 26 trial reports name no registry
entry in any source here and are held in pending registers, excluded from that
total.


## Verification

Every number here was checked against its source by verifiers instructed to
**refute rather than confirm**. 614 verdicts over 11 batches, sampled in strata
weighted to risk (every hydrocephalus row, every grade-3 row, all
figure-digitised values, the largest lane): 229 CONFIRMED, 223 CONFIRMED_MINOR,
**149 REFUTED**, 13 UNVERIFIABLE. 253 rows were corrected and the canonical
tables regenerated.

Two results are worth knowing before using the data:

- **One number cited a real regulatory document and appeared nowhere in it.** An
  exposure duration of "median 119.4wk (range 4-212wk)" was attributed to the
  Qalsody EPAR across 17 rows; full-text extraction of that 181-page document
  returns zero hits for either figure. Corrected to the EPAR's actual 148.4 weeks
  / 245 weeks maximum. This is the failure mode the no-fabrication policy exists
  for, and internal consistency checking would never have caught it.
- **Grade 3 fell from 199 rows to 133** after re-grading against each scale's own
  wording. The dominant error was anchoring a grade-3 cutoff to a fraction of a
  scale's numeric range rather than to what the scale says the value *means* — a
  rodent score of 4 ("does not move forward after lifting but has an upright
  posture") is not the same as 6 ("does not respond to pinch but is breathing").

`reversibility` is `not_assessed` on 2,082 of 2,540 rows. That is mostly a
correction rather than a gap: rows had been claiming `reversible` from prose that
belonged to a different cohort, or from adverse-event tables that carry no
outcome field at all. **398 rows do carry a real recovery assessment**, and most
of those come from regulatory nonclinical reviews with designated recovery
groups — the one document class in this field that routinely looks.

Full detail, including every refuted row and the verifiers' systematic findings,
is in **`VERIFICATION-CNS.md`**.

## Data model

Two normalised tables joined on `oligo_id` (full dictionary in the per-endpoint `*.schema.md`):

| File | Grain | Key |
|------|-------|-----|
| `notes/cns/corpus/cns_oligos.csv` | one row per unique oligo (identity + design predictors) | `oligo_id` (PK) |
| `notes/cns/corpus/cns_measurements.csv` | one row per oligo × model × CNS region × delivery × dose × readout | `measurement_id` (PK), `oligo_id` (FK) |

`cns_oligos.csv` uses the **identical 17-column layout** as the kidney dataset's
`oligos.csv`, so the two can be unioned or compared without re-mapping.
`cns_measurements.csv` adds `cns_region`, `endpoint_domain`, `challenge_priority`
and `reversibility`, and grades on **`neurotox_grade` 0–3** (rubric in the per-endpoint `*.schema.md`).
Six further columns are *derived* from those by
`scripts/classify_evidence_cns.py` — `evidence_class`, `trial_key`,
`trial_key_basis`, `ascertainment`, `negative_eligible` and `event_cluster`.
They read no new document, so deleting and regenerating them loses nothing.

A denormalised analysis view is generated at `notes/cns/corpus/oligotox_cns_merged.csv`
(one row per measurement carrying both predictors and outcome). The two
normalised tables remain the source of truth; the merged file is regenerated by
`scripts/build_merged_cns.py` and never hand-edited.

## What is distinctive about this dataset

**Matched in-vitro / in-vivo pairs — animal to animal, and said so.**
**141 compounds** carry both a *mouse* intracerebroventricular acute-tolerability
score and a calcium-oscillation score in *rat* primary cortical neurons — same
molecules, published sequences, both arms, from one source
(`doi:10.1089/nat.2021.0071`, 181 in-vivo rows and 176 in-vitro rows).

This is **animal-to-animal pairing, and it is not the bridge the challenge asks
for.** An earlier version of this file put the brief's request for data that can
*"bridge the differences between predictions that are primarily based on data from
animal-based studies to data collected by in vitro human-based systems"* beside
this panel, and gave the count as 181 compounds in both arms. Both were wrong: the
in-vitro arm is rat, not human, and 181 is the number of in-vivo rows, while the
number of molecules measured in *both* arms is 141. The correction came from
Beebop's 2026-09-30 review of the acute module and is recorded in
[`ROCKSTEADY_RESPONSE_TO_BEEBOP_2026-09-30.md`](./acute-neurotoxicity/ROCKSTEADY_RESPONSE_TO_BEEBOP_2026-09-30.md).

What the corpus can actually say about bridging systems, computed by
`scripts/cross_system_pairs_cns.py` rather than asserted:

| Molecules measured in both… | Count |
|---|---:|
| animal in vivo **and** animal laboratory | 193 |
| animal in vivo **and** human clinical | 12 |
| **human laboratory and animal laboratory** | **2** |
| **human laboratory and animal in vivo** | **0** |
| **human laboratory and human clinical** | **1** |

The two human-laboratory/animal-laboratory molecules are APOE ASO-1 and
TREM2 ASO-171, each assayed in human iPSC-derived microglia and human whole blood
ex vivo *and* on a rat primary-neuron multi-electrode array. The single
human-laboratory/human-clinical molecule is **valeriasen**, an n-of-1 ASO tested
in the patient's own *KCNT1* p.R474H iPSC-derived neurons and then given to that
patient. It is the most interesting record in the corpus and the least
comfortable: the human in-vitro assay found no injury (grade 0 on off-target
transcriptomics and neurite morphology) and the patient went on to grade-3 raised
intracranial pressure and status dystonicus. One molecule is not evidence about
predictive transfer in either direction, but it is the honest shape of what a
human in-vitro-to-human-clinical pair looks like here, and it is a discordant one.

**A reversibility contrast between two approved intrathecal ASOs.** Regulatory
nonclinical reviews with designated recovery groups show that hippocampal
neuronal vacuolation from one drug *persists* unchanged through 12- and 26-week
recovery, while the same lesion from another is *absent* after a 13-week
recovery. Same route, same class, opposite recovery behaviour — which is the
chronic-versus-transient distinction the challenge's named endpoint turns on, and
it is only visible because those studies included a recovery arm.

**Matched design series.** Patent-derived panels contain compounds differing in
exactly one design variable. The dataset contains, for instance, two compounds
sharing an identical 20-mer nucleobase sequence and differing only in backbone
(full-PS versus PS/PO mix), where the mixed-backbone version scores lower on
every matched CNS readout.

**Direction is modelled, not flattened.** Neurofilament light is both an efficacy
and a toxicity biomarker in this field. A *fall* after a SOD1- or HTT-lowering ASO
is intended pharmacology and grades 0; a *treatment-emergent rise* is
neuro-axonal injury and grades 2+. No NfL row is permitted to leave
`effect_direction` unset.

**Acute and chronic stay separable.** `endpoint_domain`, `exposure_duration` and
`reversibility` keep the acute hybridisation-independent behavioural syndrome
distinct from chronic neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration, rather than
collapsing both into one "neurotoxic" flag.

## Provenance & licensing

Every row is traceable to a **canonical source identifier** (NCT number, US
patent number, DOI or PMID) plus the **exact locus** — table, figure, label
section, claim, or API path — in `source_ref` and `source_table`.

`redistribution` is tracked per row and is **read from the licence statement
inside the archived source document**, not assumed:

| Rights | Rows | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `public_domain` | 1,604 | US patents, FDA/EMA documents, ClinicalTrials.gov — reproduce freely |
| `cc_by` | 555 | CC-BY source; raw values reproducible with attribution |
| `summary_stat` | 305 | Facts quoted from a source whose licence does not permit bulk reproduction |
| `verify` | 76 | Rights unresolved — settle before public release |

CC-BY-**NC** and CC-BY-**ND** sources are deliberately *not* upgraded to `cc_by`,
since republishing their tables inside a CC-BY dataset would conflict with the
non-commercial and no-derivatives terms.

**Sequences are never guessed.** `sequence_5to3` is `TBD` unless read from a
document fetched during curation. Case is significant: uppercase marks a
modified wing, lowercase the DNA gap.

## Reproducing the dataset

```bash
python scripts/ingest_calcium_panel.py   # one deterministic lane from an archived xlsx
python scripts/assemble_cns.py           # merge lane JSONs -> canonical tables
python scripts/qc_cns.py                 # schema, FK, range, provenance, endpoint policy
python scripts/build_merged_cns.py       # regenerate the analysis view
python scripts/dataset_stats_cns.py      # regenerate the statistics in the docs
```

Lane outputs are retained under `notes/cns/extractions/`, source documents under
`sources/cns/`, so derivations re-run without network access.

## Honest limitations

- **Grades are provisional**, flagged `grade_provisional`, pending
  subject-matter review.
- **Hydrocephalus is thinly sourced and must not be overstated.** The evidence is
  almost entirely clinical. The dataset now carries the untreated-disease baseline
  as data rather than prose: in a matched cohort studied entirely *before*
  nusinersen was approved — so nobody was oligonucleotide-exposed by construction
  — spinal muscular atrophy itself carries an incidence-rate ratio of **4.7
  (95% CI 2.4–10.2)** for hydrocephalus. Any ventriculomegaly row in an SMA
  patient has to be read against that.

  The mechanistic floor is close to empty, and the one animal study that looks
  like an exception does not survive reading. An ICV antisense against Gαi2 does
  dilate rat ventricles — but its own base-composition-matched mismatch control
  produced no effect, the dilatation was strictly unilateral, the molecule is an
  **unmodified phosphodiester DNA 18-mer** sharing no chemistry with any clinical
  ASO, and the effect required continuous minipump infusion where a single bolus
  did nothing. It supports "knocking down Gαi2 dilates rat ventricles". It does
  not support "intrathecal ASOs cause hydrocephalus", and the row says so.

  The tominersen exposure-dependent ventricular-volume finding, which has a
  concurrent placebo arm, remains the better anchor.
- **Human in vitro data is scarce**, and that is a finding rather than an
  omission: systematic searching found almost no study whose *purpose* is
  oligonucleotide toxicity in human iPSC-derived neural cells. Nearly every human
  in vitro row was mined from the tolerability arms of efficacy papers. Closing
  that gap is exactly what this challenge exists to do.
- **Source concentration.** A large share of rows comes from a few high-yield
  documents, so errors there would propagate; those sources were prioritised for
  verification.
- **Modality skew.** CNS-delivered oligonucleotides are overwhelmingly gapmers and
  splice-switching ASOs, and the table reflects that.

**`NEXT-STEPS-CNS.md`** turns those limitations into a gap analysis: what the
published literature is actually missing, confirmed by searching for it and
finding nothing, and what a Data Generation phase should therefore fund. The
headline is that the literature is richest exactly where the challenge assigns
lowest priority and close to empty where it assigns highest.

disciplines, and quality-control results, and `sources/SOURCES-CNS.md` for the
