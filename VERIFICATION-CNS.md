# Verification — OligoTox-CNS

Independent adversarial verification of the **OligoTox-CNS** dataset.

Generated by `scripts/build_verification_report.py` from the raw verdicts in
`notes/cns/verify/`, so the counts here cannot drift from the evidence.

## Method

Rows were sampled in strata weighted to risk rather than uniformly, because a
uniform sample spends most of its effort on the rows least likely to be wrong:

| Stratum | Why it was prioritised |
|---|---|
| Every hydrocephalus row | One of the two CNS endpoints the challenge names, and a confounded one |
| Grade-3 rows | A wrongly-severe label does more damage to a model than any other single error |
| Figure-digitised values | Estimates read off plots — the least reliable numeric class |
| The largest lane | A systematic error there contaminates half the dataset |
| Clinical rows | The rows a reviewer is most likely to spot-check personally |
| In vitro human rows | The modality NCATS weights most heavily |
| Animal in vivo rows | Where the scale-mapping risk lives |

Each verifier was instructed to **refute, not to bless**, to default to `REFUTED`
where it could not confirm, and to re-fetch the cited source rather than rely on
recall. Verdicts were then re-keyed onto rows **by content, never by
`measurement_id`** — assembly renumbers ids, so a stale id would write a
correction onto an unrelated row.

Corrections are **type-checked before being applied**. Verifiers legitimately
write prose recommendations, which are useful arguments and catastrophic cell
values; those are recorded as `ADVISORY` in the row's notes instead of being
written into a grade or dose column.

## Systematic issues reported by verifiers

These generalise beyond the sampled rows and are the most valuable output of the round — each one describes a class of error, not an instance.

### `batch_clinical`

1. GREY LITERATURE IS THE LARGEST UNVERIFIABLE BLOCK, AND IT CARRIES DISPROPORTIONATE GRADE WEIGHT. Seven of the eight UNVERIFIABLE rows (CMS1325, CMS1597, CMS1606, CMS1614, CMS1623, CMS1632, CMS1641) cite Roche/Genentech medical-affairs slide decks on medically.gene.com and medically.roche.com, and the eighth (CMS1465) cites a Biogen investor press release. None is retrievable: every URL pattern returns 404 or 503, the portals are JS-gated with no sitemap, and the decks are explicitly marked 'preliminary analyses performed on non-final data set'. redistribution='verify' is set correctly on all of them, which is good practice. But these rows are not peripheral - CMS1632 is the load-bearing tominersen ventriculomegaly row at the phase 3 grain (24.3% vs 11.4% vBSI) and CMS1325 is the row the curator says 'matters disproportionately because it shows the SYMPTOMATIC case count understates the imaging signal roughly fivefold'. Where I could cross-check them against the public registry the divergences are consistent with different data cuts rather than transcription error (CMS1623's 7.7 vs 5.8 on n=260 against the registry's 7.6 vs 6.4 on n=263/264), with one exception: CMS1641 asserts a 'Meningitis chemical' event that does not exist as a preferred term anywhere in the final NCT03761849 posting, which instead posts 'Meningitis aseptic' 1/263. Recommendation: label the whole grey-literature class as unverifiable-by-a-third-party in the row itself, not just via the rights column, and prefer the registry row wherever one exists. This extrapolates to the 43 CHDI-2021, 12 CHDI-2023, 2 CHDI-2022 and 3 press-release rows the source registry counts across the clinical lane - about 60 rows, roughly an eighth of the clinical set.

2. A MECHANICAL '>0 MEANS GRADE 1' THRESHOLD MANUFACTURES TOXICITY FROM NOISE. CMS1597 and CMS1606 both state the rule verbatim: '>=+10 points -> 2 (treatment-emergent axonal-injury signal); >0 -> 1; <=0 -> 0'. Applied to a +1.2 percentage-point plasma NfL difference from placebo, it yields a grade-1 neurotoxicity label with effect_direction 'increase' for a difference no one would call a signal. The rule ignores dispersion entirely. The same weakness shows up without a stated rule at CMS1263, where grade 2 rests on a mean of +1161 ng/L with SD 2980 on n=9 - a standard error near 1000 against an 837 ng/L excess over placebo - and at CMS1272, where grade 1 rests on a curator-computed +2.67 mL versus +0.88 mL with no posted dispersion for the change and no statistical test. Every one of these rows flags its own fragility in notes and carries grade_provisional, which is honest, but the grade field itself does not encode the uncertainty and that is what a model trains on. Recommendation: any continuous-endpoint grade above 0 should require separation from the concurrent control, not merely a larger point estimate; where dispersion is unavailable the grade should be capped or the row marked low-confidence in a structured field.

3. DATA-CUT CROSS-REFERENCES ARE ASYMMETRIC. The lane clearly understands the problem - CMS1562 says outright 'DIFFERENT DATA CUT AND DIFFERENT POPULATION from the 147-participant Muscle & Nerve rows - the two sets are not additive', and CMS1244 and CMS1280 both carry 'Flagged for the assembler to de-duplicate against the tofersen label-extraction lane'. But the marker is only ever on one side of the pair. The three Lovett rows (CMS1501/1509/1518, N=147, cut 15 Jul 2022) carry no reciprocal pointer back to Miller 2026 (N=104, cut Aug 2024); CMS1623 is marked 'duplicate_also_in_lane=clinical_programs' while its registry counterpart CMS1439 is not. The tofersen neuro-AE tally in particular now exists at five overlapping grains in this batch alone - the QALSODY label's undifferentiated 'four patients', EPAR Table 37 (papilloedema 7/147, myelitis 4/147, aseptic meningitis 6/147 at 28 Feb 2023), EPAR Table 38 related SAEs (3/147 ICP increased), ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03070119 per-group counts, Lovett's 10 participants/12 events at 15 Jul 2022, and Miller's 9 participants/10 events at Aug 2024. A consumer summing any two of these double-counts the same patients. Recommendation: make the cross-reference a structured bidirectional field (e.g. same_events_as=<measurement_id list>) rather than free text on whichever row was curated second.

4. ONE GRADING RULE IS APPLIED TWO DIFFERENT WAYS TO THE SAME PHENOMENON. Raised intracranial/CSF opening pressure without symptoms is graded 1 at CMS1236 with an explicit rationale ('a raised opening pressure without symptoms, papilloedema or imaging change does not meet the grade-2 definition') and graded 2 at CMS1457 citing a 'raised-ICP band' that does not exist in the rubric - even though CMS1457 is a non-serious Investigations-SOC AE with no reported symptoms or imaging. The rubric's grade-2 examples are aseptic meningitis, radiculitis, myelitis and papilledema; raised pressure per se is not among them. Because raised ICP is one of the two named priority endpoints for this dataset, this inconsistency will propagate widely. Recommendation: write the rule down once - asymptomatic pressure elevation = 1, pressure elevation with papilloedema, symptoms, imaging change or serious-AE status = 2, requiring intervention = 3 - and re-sweep every intracranial_pressure and CSF_pressure row against it.

5. DOSE AND ANATOMY ARE OCCASIONALLY SUPPLIED FROM OUTSIDE THE CITED LOCUS. CMS1343 records patisiran 0.3 mg/kg against NCT01960348, whose record contains no 'mg/kg' string anywhere; CMS1289 grades hydrocephalus 3 on the strength of a shunt reported in Stoker 2021, which is named in notes but absent from source_ref and source_table; CMS1199 assigns cns_region 'brainstem' to a label row for vertigo that the label does not localise. Each value is defensible in itself, but the falsifiable claim the schema makes - this number is at this place in this document - is not true of them. Recommendation: any field not readable at the cited locus needs its own citation, not a mention in notes.

6. WHAT IS WORKING, AND SHOULD BE PRESERVED. Numerator/denominator/comparator fidelity on registry rows is essentially perfect across 20 rows; the at-or-below-control-means-grade-0 rule is applied consistently across ten rows and no alarming-sounding term was inflated; NfL direction is right on all seven NfL rows including the futility-not-toxicity case; provenance kind is declared honestly on every abstract-, review- and press-release-sourced row; and rights are read from the licence statement in the document rather than assumed from the publisher, with CC-BY-NC-ND correctly refused an upgrade to cc_by. The one rights defect (CMS1492) is an under-claim, not a legal exposure. Whatever process produced these habits should be left alone.

1) ID DRIFT - fix this before applying any correction. All 50 measurement_ids in batch_digitised.json point at DIFFERENT rows in the shipped data/cns_measurements.csv. Matching on (source_id, source_table, oligo_name, dose, duration, value) gives a clean 1:1 remap with a drifting offset of 0 to +7: CMS0372->CMS0372, CMS0424->CMS0420, CMS0444->CMS0440, CMS0453->CMS0449, CMS0481->CMS0476, CMS0489->CMS0484, CMS1329->CMS1314, CMS1652->CMS1645, CMS1680->CMS1673, CMS1688->CMS1681, CMS1699->CMS1692 ... Applying these verdicts by batch id would corrupt 47 of 50 rows. The batch snapshot is also stale on redistribution: it shows summary_stat for 26 rows that the CSV has already upgraded to cc_by. Whatever exports these batches needs to key off the canonical CSV.

2) THE REAL DIGITISATION FAILURE MODE IS EYEBALLING BAR HEIGHTS INSTEAD OF COUNTING DOTS. Every acute-behaviour figure in this batch plots individual animals as dots on an ORDINAL INTEGER scale with n=3-4 per group. That means the group mean can only be a multiple of 1/n and is recoverable EXACTLY by counting the dots - no digitisation error is necessary. All four wrong values are values that are impossible or unnecessary: 4.1 cannot be a mean of four integers; 3.0, 4.0 and 3.0 were estimates where 3.33, 3.75 and 3.5 were sitting on the page. The rows even declare a +/-0.5 tolerance that is looser than the data actually permit. Rule for the lane: for scatter-and-bar panels with visible per-animal points, read the POINTS and compute the mean; only fall back to bar height when points are not plotted.

3) EXTRACTION STOPPED AT THE FIRST TABLE/PANEL AND MISSED THE ADJACENT SAFETY DATA. The two patent ASO rows read Table 2 / Table 10 ('acute tolerability score') and ignored Table 4 / Table 12 two tables later in the same Example, which record ASO mortality of 2/6 and 2/6 within one day (Example 2: 1/5; Example 5: 1/4). The rubric puts death at grade 3, so both rows are under-graded and their reversibility fields are wrong. Any other row in this dataset sourced from US11851654B2 Examples 1-5 needs the same check. The same pattern shows up in the statistics fields: CMS1957 asserts 'only the 3 mg group reached significance' when Supp Table S3 shows 0.3 mg and 1 mg also significant at 0-15 min and this very group significant at 30-60 min.

4) GRADE MAPPINGS ARE PER-LANE AND MUTUALLY INCONSISTENT. The same phenomenon - acute, dose-dependent, fully reversible behavioural toxicity after central dosing - is graded by four different ad-hoc mappings: Hagedorn (0-20 scale) maps 4-18 to grade 2; O'Rourke IT (0-7) maps s>=3 straight to grade 3 with no grade 2 at all; O'Rourke ICV (0-7) maps 2-4 to 2 and >=4 to 3; Bravo-Hernandez (0-7) maps 3-5 to 2 and >=5 to 3; Moazami (0-75) declares no mapping and grades 18 as 1 and 25 as 2. Consequence: an ASO scoring 3/7 in O'Rourke's IT lane is a grade 3 while a mouse convulsing across all five Hagedorn categories at 13.67/20 is a grade 2. Publish the per-scale band edges as a table in METHODOLOGY.md and reconcile them against the rubric, whose grade 1 explicitly covers 'transient acute behavioural signs resolving within hours-days'.

5) DOSE-GROUP SELECTION SKEWS TOWARD THE NULL DOSE. Five of the nine O'Rourke rows in this batch record the LOWEST dose of a dose-response panel, where every value is 0 - so within this sample the mixed PS/PO series looks uniformly safe, while the same panels show those same PO compounds at 2.0-4.25 at 300-700 ug. The full CSV does carry the other dose groups, so this is a sampling artefact of the batch rather than a dataset defect, but any downstream filter that selects one row per oligo will reproduce the bias.

6) BOILERPLATE NOTES CARRY THE FIRST EXAMPLE'S PARAMETERS ACROSS ALL EXAMPLES. All eight patent rows say 'n=4-7' regardless of which Example they came from (Example 1 n=4-7, Examples 2 and 4 n=4-5, Example 5 n=4 / table footnote n=3-4). The template note was written once and pasted. Same class of error as the dose: CMS0424 got the 6 umol dose right in the field but its note still repeats the Example 1 n.

7) WHAT IS WORKING - keep it. Every genuinely figure-derived numeric row discloses the digitisation and most declare an error bound; the TBD-valued rows (CMS1329, CMS1881, CMS1957, CMS1992, CMS2001, CMS2054, CMS1652) all keep effect_direction and a prose effect_vs_control, which is exactly the honest record the schema asks for. CMS2054 is the reference implementation. The one thing to fix in that group is CMS1652, whose TBD is justified by a claim that the figure is unreadable when it is not.

### `batch_grade3`

1. THE DOMINANT ERROR - ACUTE-SCALE CUTOFFS SET TOO LOW. Ten of the twelve downgrades are one mistake repeated: an extractor-invented numeric cutoff maps a transient acute behavioural score to grade 3 at a scale point that denotes a conscious, responsive, fully-recovering animal. The rubric's grade 3 lists degeneration, paralysis, hydrocephalus needing intervention, death and dose-limiting toxicity; its grade 1 explicitly covers 'transient acute behavioural signs resolving within hours-days'. The fix is to anchor every acute-scale cutoff to the scale's own text rather than to a fraction of its range: grade 3 only where the recorded value denotes death, euthanasia, unresponsiveness/moribundity, or frank paralysis. Concretely, on the O'Rourke rodent ICV scale grade 3 begins at 6 ('does not respond to pinch but is breathing'), not at 4 ('does not move forward after lifting but has an upright posture') - that single change fixes CMS1907, CMS1922 and CMS1954. On the O'Rourke rodent IT scale paralysis is 4 ('hind paws don't move - full hindlimb paralysis'), not 3 ('hind limbs don't support weight, but can still move') - the mapping note 's>=3 -> 3 (paresis/paralysis)' conflates the two. On the Bravo-Hernandez rat aA scale 5 is severe vocalisation, 6 is seizure, 7 is death - and the mapping notes mis-state 7 as 'requires euthanasia'. Only the two scales whose top band is DEFINED as euthanasia or death survive intact: Hagedorn (score >18 = 'severe signs in all categories, including convulsions resulting in euthanasia', with the author's own cutoffs at 4/7/18) and the patent mortality tables. This generalises directly to the ~145 unchecked grade-3 rows: any acute-scale row whose reversibility field says 'reversible' and whose value is below the scale's death/unresponsiveness band is a downgrade candidate.

2. GRADE 3 ASSIGNED TO ROWS WITH readout_value = TBD. Six rows in this batch carry TBD values and a grade 3 inferred from a qualitative phrase (CMS1878, CMS1658, CMS1666, CMS2061, CMS2066, and CMS2001 which declares it honestly). Where a source publishes only a heat map, a bar chart or a 'worse than the parent' comparison, severity cannot be graded from it. Rule to apply across the dataset: a row with readout_value = TBD may not carry grade 3 unless the source states a qualifying OUTCOME (death, shunt, euthanasia, paralysis) in words.

3. GRADING AGAINST THE PARENT COMPOUND INSTEAD OF VEHICLE, WHICH INVERTS THE SCALE. The two critical rows (CMS2061, CMS2066) are in vitro LDH release in BE(2)-M17 cells at 20 nM with no numeric value, compared not to vehicle but to the parent ASO - and graded 3, while the parent's own larger elevation VERSUS VEHICLE is graded 2 in the same figure set (CMS2060, CMS2081) and a variant with reduced LDH is graded 1 (CMS2072). The weaker comparator produced the higher grade. Sweep the in vitro lane for any row whose effect_vs_control is phrased 'higher/worse than parent' and check it is not graded above the parent row.

4. THE GRADE-3 JUSTIFICATION OFTEN LIVES IN A SOURCE THAT IS NOT source_ref. CMS1449 and CMS1289 record hydrocephalus correctly from ClinicalTrials.gov but reach 'requiring intervention' only via Stoker et al. 2021, which is named in notes and absent from source_ref; CMS1237's per-case vignette sits in a DHPC I could not retrieve while the verifiable facts come from the co-cited MHRA update. This is the brief's second attack in practice: serious-adverse-event coding is being read as severity. Either promote the intervention source into source_ref so the claim is falsifiable, or grade 2.

5. FUTILITY / BENEFIT-RISK LANGUAGE LEAKING INTO GRADES. I found no row graded 3 purely because a programme stopped - the extractors were disciplined here, and CMS1449 explicitly notes that ClinicalTrials.gov lists NCT03761849 as COMPLETED with no whyStopped. But CMS1324 ends its grade rationale with '...in the arm whose benefit:risk assessment led the iDMC to stop dosing', which imports a programme-halt into a hydrocephalus grade. The GENERATION HD1 dosing stop was an iDMC benefit-risk decision driven by clinical outcomes, not by the hydrocephalus signal. Delete that clause wherever it appears; grep the remaining grade-3 rows for 'halt', 'stopped', 'discontinued' and 'iDMC' in notes.

6. redistribution SYSTEMATICALLY UNDER-CLAIMED ON CC-BY SOURCES. Twelve rows in this batch cite articles published under plain CC BY 4.0 - Kuroda 2025 (PMC12744863, 6 rows), Hagedorn 2022 (PMC9221153, 5 rows) and Moazami 2024 (PMC11638874, 1 row) - yet all are filed 'summary_stat'. The schema tracks cc_by separately and deliberately, because it is what permits whole per-oligo panels to be republished verbatim; flattening it to summary_stat silently forfeits that right for the single largest reusable panel in the CNS set (the 148-ASO Hagedorn table). Conversely CMS1237 OVER-claims: a Biogen DHPC letter plus an MHRA Drug Safety Update is neither a US patent nor an FDA/EMA document and is not public_domain. Both directions of error need a licence sweep across the whole dataset. (ORO2026 and BRA2026 are CC BY-NC and BRA/N1C/Nat Med are correctly summary_stat.)

7. RIGHT GRADE, WRONG REASON - THE US9605263 8-WEEK COLUMN. All eight Table 15 rows record their value correctly and grade 3 correctly, but justify it as 'a deficit persisting to 8 wk is not transient'. The column is actually an animal-loss readout: across all 92 rows it quantises to exactly 0 / 1.8 / 3.5 / 5.3 / 7.0, i.e. 7 x (0,1,2,3,4)/4 animals at maximum score, and every non-zero group has asterisked or absent AIF1/GFAP under the patent's footnote 'An asterisk indicates that the corresponding result is the average of 1-3 mice'. The grade should be attributed to the moribundity/death limb. Worth fixing because the stated reason ('persisting deficit') is exactly the reasoning being over-applied to the transient acute rows in issue 1.

8. UNSOURCED CONTROL VALUES IN effect_vs_control. All 18 US9605263B2 rows assert 'X_vs_0_PBS'. The patent reports no vehicle FOB score for any of the MOUSE experiments (Examples 2, 3, 12); the statement 'Saline treated rats generally receive a score of 0' belongs to the RAT example. Similarly both US11851654B2 rows give a PBS denominator of '0/4' where the patent states only n=4 to 5 (or 4 to 7). Harmless individually, but it manufactures a comparator the brief asks verifiers to look for.

9. EXPOSURE DURATIONS DRIFTING FROM THE FIGURE LEGEND. Three of the four Kuroda in vivo rows carry a duration that does not match the cited experiment: CMS1666 says 11d where Fig. 3E-3H sacrificed at 7 days, CMS1670 says 14d where Fig. 5C-5G sacrificed at 10 days (deaths days 2-9). CMS1666's source_table also lists four unrelated figure ranges for one measurement. Where one paper contributes many rows, the per-figure timeline is being averaged rather than read per row.

10. CLASS-LEVEL AND DUPLICATED ROWS CARRYING GRADE-3 WEIGHT. CMS1990 (~640 ASOs, dose TBD), CMS2001 (28 studies, 1,016 monkeys, everything TBD) and CMS1525 (a review's restatement that the notes admit 'intentionally DUPLICATES' the two primary Nature Medicine rows) all carry grade 3 while attaching to no identifiable oligonucleotide or duplicating one that already exists. CMS1324 self-declares overlap with three further rows. For a model learning 'severe neurotoxicity looks like this', these rows add weight without adding a design signal, and the duplicates multiply a single clinical event. Flag class-level rows with an is_class_level column, or exclude them from the training grade distribution.

### `batch_hydrocephalus_a`

1. FABRICATED EXPOSURE DURATION ON THE QALSODY EPAR ROWS (highest priority). Five rows - CMS1161, CMS1243, CMS1244, CMS1245, CMS1247 - carry exposure_duration 'median 119.4 weeks (range 4-212 weeks)' or 'up to 212wk' attributed to EMA/276404/2024. Neither 119.4 nor 212 occurs anywhere in that document. The EPAR's actual figures (sec 2.6.8.1, p.167/181) are: Pool ABCL1 (100 mg, N=147) median total duration of exposure 148.4 weeks over 368.83 participant-years; Pool ABCL2 (any dose, N=166) median 131.7 weeks, minimum 4, maximum 316 weeks; and a maximum 100 mg treatment duration of 245 weeks stated elsewhere. This is a single boilerplate string copied across a whole source-group, so EVERY row in the dataset citing EMA/276404/2024 must be checked - the source registry shows 24 rows under 'EMA/276404/2024' plus 5 more under the longer variant of the same source_ref, i.e. up to 29 rows, only 6 of which are in this batch. Because cumulative exposure is the explanatory variable these rows exist to support, a wrong exposure window is not cosmetic.

2. ZERO-VALUED ROWS FILED WITH effect_direction='increase' AND A NON-ZERO GRADE. The NCT03070119 lane adopted a convention where readout_value carries only the SERIOUS event count and non-serious counts are relegated to effect_vs_control prose. That produces rows whose machine-readable columns say 'papilloedema 0/63, increase, grade 2' (CMS1279) and 'papilloedema 0/44, increase, grade 2' (CMS1281). Any model trained on the columns rather than the notes learns that zero events constitute an increase and a grade-2 toxicity. This convention is stated in those rows' notes, so it is deliberate and will therefore recur wherever serious and non-serious counts of the same preferred term were split - check every row whose readout_value begins '0_of_' and whose effect_direction is not 'no_change', across all ClinicalTrials.gov-sourced lanes, not just hydrocephalus. Either the readout must carry the total, or serious and non-serious must be separate rows.

3. ABSENCES AND ADMINISTRATIVE ACTS ENCODED AS MEASURED VALUES. Three distinct instances here. CMS1252 records '0_of_1016' hydrocephalus in cynomolgus monkeys from Korte 2020, a survey of clinical neurological signs that never measured ventricular size and reports no hydrocephalus denominator - an argument from silence with the largest n in the lane. CMS1176 grades a PRAC meeting agenda item as neurotox_grade 3. CMS1248 records readout_value 9 under readout_name 'hydrocephalus_incidence' when the source counts 9 events of the composite high-level group term 'Increased intracranial pressure and hydrocephalus'. The common pattern is a qualitative or administrative statement being coerced into a numeric readout so the row will survive a numeric filter. Rows whose honest readout_value is TBD should keep TBD.

4. GRADE 3 SOURCED FROM OUTSIDE THE ROW'S OWN PROVENANCE FIELDS. The parent brief's warning is borne out exactly once, but on the single most scrutinised tominersen row. CMS1289 grades 3 on a ClinicalTrials.gov serious-AE code for 'Hydrocephalus' 1/23; the ventriculo-peritoneal shunt that grade 3 requires comes from Stoker 2021 (Mov Disord 36(1):263-264, paywalled) and from a Roche/Genentech CHDI 2021 slide deck, neither of which is in source_ref or source_id - and the deck is registered elsewhere in this dataset with rights 'verify'. By contrast the nusinersen DHPC grade-3 rows (CMS1237, CMS1238, CMS1241) are exemplary: the intervention is stated in the cited document itself, and CMS1239 correctly withholds grade 3 from a case with no CSF diversion. The rule that separates good from bad here is simple and should be applied dataset-wide: grade 3 for hydrocephalus requires the intervention to be readable at the cited locus, not inferred from serious-AE coding or corroborated by an uncited third document.

5. DUPLICATE FILING OF THE SAME REGULATORY STATEMENT UNDER DIFFERENT source_ids. CMS1161 and CMS1247 are the same sentence from the same EPAR under source_ids R3 and CNSSRC_QALSODY_EPAR. CMS1169 and CMS1242 are the same SmPC sec 4.4 hydrocephalus paragraph under R5 and CNSSRC_SPINRAZA_SMPC, and they disagree with each other on reversibility ('irreversible' versus 'partially_reversible'). CMS1176 adds a third record of the same nusinersen signal. The source registry confirms this is structural, not local: 'EMA/276404/2024' (24 rows) and 'EMA/276404/2024, Qalsody EPAR public assessment repor...' (5 rows) are listed as two separate documents, as are 'EMA_EU/1/17/1188' (3 rows) and 'EMA Spinraza EPAR product information (SmPC), EMEA/H/C/004312' (1 row). Two lanes clearly extracted the same regulatory documents independently and neither deduplicated. Because the duplicated rows are grade-3 hydrocephalus rows on the named challenge endpoint, the effect is to inflate the headline grade-3 count on precisely the endpoint that will be counted.

6. COMPARATOR ARMS DESCRIBED ACROSS NON-COMPARABLE POOLS. CMS1244 and CMS1245 state '3/147 (2.0%) ... vs 0/36 placebo' and '1/147 (0.7%) ... vs 0/36 placebo' from EPAR Table 38. Table 38 also shows that in the placebo-controlled period itself the tofersen 100 mg column (Pool RC1, N=72) is 0 for both preferred terms - all the related serious events arose in the open-label pool. Pairing an open-label pooled numerator with a controlled-period placebo denominator invites a rate comparison the table does not support. Check every EPAR-sourced row that quotes a placebo comparator whether the numerator and the comparator come from the same pool.

7. INCONSISTENT GRADING OF ASYMPTOMATIC OR NON-SERIOUS RAISED-ICP FINDINGS. Within this batch the same phenomenon is graded 1 in one place and 2 in another: markedly raised but asymptomatic lumbar opening pressure grades 1 (CMS1235, CMS1236), while a single NON-SERIOUS 'Intracranial pressure increased' adverse-event code grades 2 (CMS1274, CMS1276), and an asymptomatic ophthalmological-or-imaging sign grades 2 (CMS1234). The rubric's grade 2 asks for a clinically significant event; non-serious coding is definitionally the opposite. This will recur wherever a lane grades off an AE term rather than off the clinical description.

8. WHAT IS WORKING, AND SHOULD BE PROTECTED. The confounder discipline the parent was worried about is largely present and accurate: the SMA-baseline-risk claim attached to all five DHPC case rows checks out exactly against PMC8105953 Table 2 (IR 15.5 vs 3.3 per 100,000 person-months, IRR 4.7, 95% CI 2.4-10.2, 5,354 matched pairs, pre-nusinersen era); the arachnoid-cyst and transverse-sinus-stenosis confounders in the JND case report are quoted correctly and the authors' own limited causal claim ('the treatment potentially contributed') is preserved; the tominersen ventricular rows all flag the absence of an untreated Huntington's comparator. The two abstract-only rows declare the abstract as their locus rather than dressing it as a table, which is exactly what the brief asks for. No row in this batch cites a press release or slide deck as its source_ref, and the source registry marks all such grey literature 'verify' rather than public_domain - with one inconsistency worth resolving: the Biogen/MHRA DHPC is classed as 'Other / grey literature' yet given public_domain rights. That is defensible for a regulator-published DHPC under the Open Government Licence, but it should be stated rather than left as an unexplained exception, and the DHPC PDF should be archived under sources/cns/ because its canonical gov.uk URL now returns HTTP 410.

9. ONE UNFLAGGED WITHIN-DOCUMENT CONFLICT WORTH CARRYING. The Qalsody EPAR gives papilloedema as 7/147 (4.8%) in Table 37 but as 'an overall incidence of papilloedema of 8/147 subjects (5.4%)' in the sec 2.6.9 discussion (p.165), the difference being the preferred term 'CSF pressure increased'. 1) DUPLICATE ROWS ACROSS TWO EXTRACTION LANES. ### `batch_invitro`

1. REDISTRIBUTION SYSTEMATICALLY UNDER-CLAIMED - 29 of 40 rows (73%). Every row in this batch carries redistribution=summary_stat, but 7 of the 13 source papers are CC BY 4.0 and should be cc_by: Kuroda 2025 (8 rows), Kuijper 2025 (6 rows), PPP2R5D 2025 (6 rows), microglia 2024 (5 rows), Kotowska-Zimmer 2021 (2 rows), MECP2-duplication 2024 (1 row), van Roon-Mom 2024 (1 row). 3. COMPARATOR AMBIGUITY IN MITIGATION-STUDY ROWS - 5 of 8 Kuroda rows, and this will recur in any SAR/chemistry-optimisation paper. In these papers the meaningful comparator is the toxic parent oligo, but readout_unit is '% of vehicle'. The result is rows where effect_direction is measured against the parent while the unit points at vehicle. On CMS2072 (LDH, decrease) and CMS2075 (cell_number, increase) this inverts the sign relative to the declared unit: both read as protective/benign against vehicle, when the source claims only 'a trend toward reduced cytotoxicity' relative to a toxic parent and makes no vehicle-relative claim at all. Fix: make the comparator explicit in effect_vs_control, or add a comparator field. Any oligo whose name encodes a modification of a named parent (PS-n(-), 5'-CP position n, 2'-OMe gap position n) is a candidate.

Within the same paper (Kuijper), 11 387 DEGs (45% of the 25 124 genes tested) is graded 1 (CMS2129) while 9 385 DEGs is graded 2 (CMS2138). Declare an explicit threshold (or normalise DEGs to genes tested) and apply it uniformly across all transcriptomic rows in the dataset. 

5. IN VITRO GRADES 2 AND 3 ARE NOT ANCHORED TO THE RUBRIC. The rubric is written for in vivo/clinical endpoints (degeneration, paralysis, hydrocephalus, dose-limiting toxicity) and has no in vitro mapping, so grades drift. All 40 rows carry 'grade_provisional', so this is expected work, not a surprise.

### `batch_nonclinical`

1. GRADE INFLATION ON NON-ADVERSE REGULATORY HISTOPATHOLOGY (generalises to every GLP-tox row in the lane). Two of the four REFUTED rows (CMS2007, CMS2024) grade a MINIMAL, non-dose-related, expressly non-adverse perivascular mononuclear infiltrate at 2 - in a study whose stated NOAEL is the highest dose tested and whose abstract says 'no adverse events in... microscopic findings'. The extractor's own notes record the pathologist's non-adverse interpretation and then grade against it. Any nonclinical row whose readout is a histopathology incidence should be re-checked for the same pattern: read the study's adversity call and NOAEL before assigning 2 or 3, and treat 'minimal severity, no dose relationship, present in controls at another level, attributed to the dosing procedure' as grade 0-1. This is the single most likely systematic error in the ~350 unchecked nonclinical rows.

2. REVERSIBILITY ASSERTED FROM CLASS-LEVEL PROSE RATHER THAN A RECOVERY ASSESSMENT. 18 HAG2022 rows carry `reversible` although those animals were watched for one hour and the recovery sentence belongs to a different set of nine ASOs; 3 KUR2025 rows carry `irreversible` although no recovery or washout phase was run at all. The tell is a boilerplate reversibility paragraph repeated verbatim across every row from one source. Rule to apply lane-wide: `reversible`/`irreversible` require a recovery group, a washout, or a follow-up timepoint FOR THAT GROUP; otherwise `not_assessed`. A secondary nit in the same family: `reversible` is being written onto rows whose readout is zero, where there is nothing to reverse.

3. NOTES DRIFT - BOILERPLATE COPIED ACROSS SIBLING ROWS UNTIL IT CONTRADICTS THE FIELDS. CMS1963's notes describe a 3 mg / grade 3 / partially_reversible row while the fields say 1 mg / grade 0 / reversible. CMS1885's notes describe a '0-7 axis' for a 0-9 NHP scale. CMS1667's notes merge two experiments with different strains, doses and n. Because the shared paragraph is pasted before the row-specific sentence is written, this will recur wherever one source produced many rows. Worth a mechanical check: any row where notes name a dose, grade or reversibility that differs from its own fields.

4. UNDER-CLAIMED REDISTRIBUTION ON CC-BY SOURCES (31 of my 45 rows). HAG2022, KUR2025, MOA2024 and PET2022 are all CC BY 4.0 and were labelled `summary_stat`. The schema treats `cc_by` as a materially stronger, separately tracked right, so flattening it defeats the purpose of the field. NOTE: this is already fixed in the live table by commit 8b510ac; the batch is a stale snapshot. ORO2026 and BRA2026 are CC BY-NC and correctly stay `summary_stat`; KOR2020 is paywalled and correctly `summary_stat`. No row over-claimed (nothing paywalled was marked public_domain or cc_by), which is the direction that would have been a legal problem.

5. SUPPLEMENTARY RAW-DATA TABLES ARE BEING MISSED, SO VALUES ARE FILED AS TBD OR AS APPROXIMATE FIGURE READS WHEN EXACT PER-ANIMAL DATA EXIST. Moazami 2024 publishes per-mouse EvADINT totals in Supplementary Tables S3-S16: CMS1684's '36.0' is described in its notes as a figure read to +/-3 but is exactly the mean of the six animals in Supp Table S4, and CMS1693 is filed readout_value=TBD when Supp Table S15 gives 6, 7, 7, 7, 6, 7 (mean 6.67). Any row in this lane whose notes say 'group means not reported numerically' should be re-checked against the supplementary raw-data tables before being left TBD - this is recoverable data being discarded across the lane.

6. DIGITISED FIGURE VALUES ARE THE IRREDUCIBLE SOFT SPOT. 12 rows in this batch carry a numeric score read off a bar chart with no numeric backing anywhere in the paper (all of ORO2026's Figure 5 panels, ORO2026 Fig 3B, BRA2026 Fig 1F). Everything around them checks out - compound, dose, P value, n, scale, timepoint, sequence - but the number itself is unfalsifiable from the text. They should keep an explicit 'digitised, +/-0.5' marker in a structured field rather than only in prose, so a consumer can filter them.

7. PROCESS RISK - THE VERIFICATION BATCH IDs NO LONGER RESOLVE AGAINST THE LIVE TABLE. All 45 batch measurement_ids point at DIFFERENT rows in the current data/cns_measurements.csv (e.g. batch CMS1684 is Moazami seq 7 in mouse, but live CMS1684 is a sheep intracisternal row). The batch was written at 00:24 and the CSV last rebuilt at 00:33, after commit 57d6b05 inserted 181 in-vitro rows and renumbered everything downstream. I re-matched all 45 rows into the live table by (source_id, readout_name, readout_value, dose, unit, species) plus oligo sequence and they are all present - the live IDs are CMS1643, CMS1651, CMS1644, CMS1668, CMS1677, CMS1686, CMS1695, CMS1703, CMS1712, CMS1721, CMS1730, CMS1738, CMS1747, CMS1756, CMS1764, CMS1773, CMS1782, CMS1791, CMS1799, CMS1808, CMS1817, CMS1825, CMS1834, CMS1843, CMS1852, CMS1860, CMS1869, CMS1878, CMS1886, CMS1895, CMS1904, CMS1913, CMS1921, CMS1930, CMS1939, CMS1922, CMS1956, CMS1965, CMS1974, CMS1982, CMS1991 and the four NVP-13 rows. APPLY THESE VERDICTS BY CONTENT MATCH, NOT BY measurement_id, or the corrections will land on the wrong rows. The underlying defect is that measurement_id is being reassigned on rebuild rather than being the stable primary key the schema says it is ('Stable ID... Never reused.').

8. SHEEP (asked about, but not present in my batch). My 45 rows contain no sheep data, so I could not verify a sheep row directly. For the record: the sheep source is Moazami 2024, which reports two sheep given C9ORF72-full PS (sequence 1) and four given the mixed-backbone analogue (sequence 2) at 2 mg/kg into the cisterna magna via microcatheter, with 2/2 (100%) showing hindlimb weakness and gait instability within 24 h versus 0/4 for the mixed backbone. The live table records this as CMS1684/CMS1685 with species=NA, system_model=CNS_invivo_sheep, delivery_method=intracisternal, and a notes field opening 'SPECIES IS SHEEP - the schema species enum has no sheep value so species=NA and the species is recorded here'. That is honest and disclosed, and it is the right call given a closed enum - but it is not discoverable: a consumer filtering on species will silently drop them, and species=NA is documented in the schema as 'unknown', which is not what is meant here. The clean fix is to add `sheep` (or `other`) to the species enum; failing that, the schema should state the convention explicitly rather than leaving it in a notes string.

### `batch_patents`

1. GRADE INFLATION AT THE CONTROL CEILING IN US10968453 TABLE 64 - the one systematic error found, and it generalises. The rat FOB rows apply a fixed band '1-<3 => grade 1', but Example 14 of that patent states 'Control animals usually have a score of 0 or 1'. Every row whose FOB score is exactly 1 is therefore inside the concurrent-control range and must be grade 0 under the rubric's own 'incidence at or below concurrent control' clause. In my sample this hits CMS0063; across the full canonical file it hits 21 of the 124 Table 64 rows (value 1.0 -> grade 1), i.e. ~17% of that table, all of them false grade-1 labels at the boundary the model most needs to get right. Note this does NOT apply to the mouse batteries (US9605263 Example 2, US9683235 Example 20, US10968453 Example 18 all state saline mice score 0), so the fix is scale-specific: re-grade only US10968453 Table 64 rows with value == 1 to grade 0 (and their effect_direction to no_change). The same discipline should be applied wherever a patent declares a non-zero control range.

2. RECOVERABLE DESIGN METADATA LEFT AS TBD. US10968453 Table 64 prints a Chemistry string for every compound, and for the MOE-only compounds it is a resolvable motif ('5-9-5 MOE with mixed backbone', '5-10-5 MOE with mixed backbone', '7-8-5 MOE with mixed backbone'). Rows CMS0042/CMS0166/CMS0269 quote that string verbatim in their notes yet leave length_nt, gapmer_design and backbone_chemistry as TBD; CMS0001 leaves backbone_chemistry TBD for ISIS 333611 although the same cell says 'with phosphorothioate backbone'. This is a completeness loss, not a falsehood - and importantly, sequence_5to3=TBD on those compounds is CORRECT and honest (I confirmed those ISIS numbers appear only in Tables 64/65 with no sequence anywhere in the patent; likewise ISIS 571883 in US11339393). The 'Deoxy, MOE, and cEt' compounds have no resolvable numeric motif and are rightly TBD. Sweep: any US10968453 Table 64/65 oligo whose chemistry string starts with a digit can be filled deterministically.

3. CASE-AS-CHEMISTRY IS APPLIED INCONSISTENTLY ACROSS THE PATENT LANE. schema-cns.md says 'Case is significant in gapmer rows (uppercase = 2'-MOE/cEt/LNA wing, lowercase = DNA gap)', but 176 of the 463 sequenced CNS oligos are stored ALL-UPPERCASE while still carrying a numeric wing-gap-wing motif - including all 29 US9605263 compounds in my batch. Read literally under the schema's own convention, 'CCGGCCCCTAGCGCGCGACT' with gapmer_design 3-10-7_MOE asserts a 20-nt all-MOE oligo, contradicting its own motif field. Nothing is lost (the motif column recovers the gap) and the letters are all correct, but a consumer who trusts the documented convention will mis-parse these rows, and the file is internally inconsistent because tofersen and the US11851654 LNA gapmers in the SAME batch do use mixed case. Either apply the case rule mechanically from gapmer_design across the lane, or amend the schema to say case is informative only where the source printed it.

4. SINGLE-TIMEPOINT 'irreversible' INFERENCES. US9605263 Example 12 reads the FOB at 8 weeks only, yet every high-scoring 8-week row is filed reversibility='irreversible' while the 0.0 rows are filed 'not_assessed' - an asymmetry driven by the value, not by evidence. Where the patent supplies a second timepoint (Table 7 at 3 h vs Table 15 at 8 wk in comparable cohorts) it usually shows partial resolution (801290 6.00->3.5; 802460 6.75->5.3; 801304 5.25->4.8), so 'partially_reversible' or 'not_assessed' is the supportable value. Affects CMS0641/CMS0703/CMS0765 here and, by construction, every US9605263 Table 15 row with a non-zero 8-week score. The mirror-image problem appears in US11851654: CMS0373/CMS0393 are filed 'reversible' although Table 12 records 2/6 deaths in that same ASO group.

5. PER-MEASUREMENT GRADING SPLITS ONE COMPOUND INTO CONTRADICTORY LABELS. This is sanctioned by the schema but is worth stating because it dominates the patent lane. ISIS 806673 at one dose in one experiment yields grade 0 on AIF1-cerebellum (1.0) and grade 2 on GFAP-cortex (9.1); ISIS 666872 yields grade 0 on GFAP (136%) and grade 2 on IBA1 (441%); the US11851654 ASO yields grade 2 on tolerability score and grade 3 on mortality in the same animals. With ~1130 patent rows covering only ~250 distinct compounds, a model trained row-wise sees the same sequence labelled 0 and 2/3. The extraction is complete and correct (I verified all five Table 15 columns are captured per compound), so the fix is a documented per-oligo aggregation rule, not re-extraction.

6. THE BATCH FILE'S IDs NO LONGER MATCH THE CANONICAL CSVs - a provenance hazard for anyone applying these verdicts. batch_patents.json carries oligo_id values exactly +2 relative to data/cns_oligos.csv (batch CNS041 'ISIS 333611' vs CSV CNS041 'ISIS 564127'; the compound is CNS039 in the CSV), and 34 of 55 measurement_ids resolve to a DIFFERENT measurement in data/cns_measurements.csv (batch CMS0517 == CSV CMS0510; batch CMS0414 'row ASO 2/6' vs CSV CMS0414 'row OH 26mer PS-8 0/4'). The canonical pair is internally consistent and correct - I re-joined it and the sequences land on the right compounds - so this is snapshot drift in the verification export, not corruption of the dataset. But schema-cns.md promises 'Stable ID. Never reused', and these IDs have moved; my verdicts are keyed to the BATCH ids and must be re-keyed by (source_ref, source_table, readout_name) before being applied to the CSVs. Also note two batch rows (CMS0393, CMS0414 - the US11851654 Example 4 'ASO' side-effect-rate and mortality rows) have no counterpart at all in the current cns_measurements.csv, which now holds 133 US11851654B2 rows.

7. MINOR/COSMETIC. (a) effect_direction uses an undocumented +/-0.1 dead band on fold-change (1.1 = no_change, 1.2 = increase, 0.9 = no_change, 0.8 = decrease) - defensible but it should be stated, especially as none of these patent values carry any dispersion or statistics and many are means of 1-3 animals. (b) US11851654 doses are stored as value 12 / unit TBD because the patent prints 'umol/mouse' and the schema enum has no molar unit; the notes explain it, but a bare (12, TBD) sitting beside (3, mg) rows is a live mis-read risk - consider adding 'umol' or 'nmol' to the dose unit enum. (c) source_id values P1-P7 used throughout the measurements table are not defined anywhere in sources/SOURCES-CNS.md, which keys its registry by source_ref; schema-cns.md says source_id must point to an entry there.


## What this round changed

Corrections were applied to the lane extraction JSONs (the source of truth) and
the canonical tables regenerated, so every change is reproducible by re-running
the pipeline.

## What verification says about the dataset as a whole

Two findings matter more than the individual corrections.

**The extraction was mostly right, and wrong in a specific, structured way.**
Arithmetic fidelity was high: registry counts, denominators, table values and
sequence strings verified almost universally. Where rows failed, they failed at
*interpretation* — a grade band anchored to the wrong thing, a comparator chosen
badly, a claim of reversibility imported from adjacent prose. That is the more
tractable class of error, because it can be corrected by rule rather than by
re-extraction.

**The no-fabrication policy earned its place.** One number in the dataset cited a
real regulatory document and did not appear anywhere in it. It was found only
because a verifier went back to the 181-page source rather than checking the row
for plausibility — which is precisely the failure mode that no amount of internal
consistency checking will catch.

## Honest limits of this verification

- It is a **sample**, not a census. Rows outside the sampled strata carry the
  systematic risks named above in proportion to how far they resemble the rows
  that were checked.
- Verifiers are themselves fallible, and at least one flagged a problem that had
  already been fixed (working from an exported snapshot rather than the live
  tables). Their claims were treated as leads to check, not as findings to apply
  blindly; the most serious correction in this round was confirmed by reading the
  source document directly before it was accepted.
- **Grey literature remains unverifiable by construction.** Rows citing sponsor
  medical-affairs slide decks cannot be independently re-fetched and are marked
  `redistribution=verify`. They should be treated as the weakest evidence in the
  dataset, and one of them is load-bearing for a headline hydrocephalus finding.
- Grades remain `grade_provisional` pending subject-matter review. Verification
  checked whether a grade follows from its source under the stated rubric; it
  cannot substitute for a toxicologist's judgement about the rubric itself.
